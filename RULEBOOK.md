## 1. Story Introduction

In the Known Universe, spice melange is the most precious resource.
Spice exists only on the desert planet Arrakis, also called Dune.
Control of spice is the focus of conflict between the Great Houses.
Houses seek alliances and support to hold their position.

These powers take part in the struggle:

- The Landsraad councilors, who govern.
- CHOAM Company, which seeks profit.
- The Bene Gesserit sisterhood, which plans far ahead.
- The Spacing Guild, which controls foldspace travel.
- The Fremen, strong warriors of the desert.
- The Emperor himself, who fights for control.

Conflict is certain. Its result is uncertain.
But one fact is certain:

> Whoever controls the spice controls the universe.

In the world of Dune, computers (thinking machines) are banned.
They have been banned for many millennia, after a war in which Artificial Intelligence almost destroyed humanity.
After that war, some humans trained to become “human calculators”. These humans are called Mentats.

## 2. Across the Imperium

This section has no game rules. You can skip it if you want.
But read it if you do not know the world of Dune.
It shows how the game board connects to the story.

DUNE: IMPERIUM is a deck-building worker placement game.
It uses elements and persons from the Dune legacy.
This includes the new film from Legendary Pictures.
It also includes the books by Frank Herbert, Brian Herbert, and Kevin J. Anderson.

Note: The game has eight leaders, two from each of four Great Houses.
For the best story, do not use two leaders from the same House in one game.
But the game also permits other combinations and “What If?” scenarios.

- **Emperor Shaddam IV of House Corrino:** He is the ruthless Emperor of the Known Universe. Work with him to gain Solari, the main currency of the Imperium.
- **Landsraad:** The Emperor leads the Landsraad. It represents the Great Houses. Solari helps you make connections, get favors, and gain advantages there.
- **CHOAM (Combine Honnete Ober Advancer Mercantiles):** It is a very large trade organization. It spans the Imperium. It will give you Solari fast, if you make a deal.
- **Spacing Guild:** It controls foldspace travel. Its large heighliners can move many troops to Dune for you, if you pay the high price in spice.
- **Populated areas of Dune:** They are important sites for military campaigns. You can collect troops at most of these locations.
- **Sandworms:** The very large sandworms rule the deserts of Dune. They are a constant danger to those who harvest spice. But you must enter the desert to get its rewards. One good harvest opens many doors.
- **Bene Gesserit:** Their intrigues and plans are everywhere in the Imperium, seen and unseen.
- **Fremen:** They are fierce warriors. Their water discipline gives them a special advantage in the fight for Dune. Their help is necessary to harvest spice.

## 4. Setup

Do these steps in order: A through H.

### A. Place components on the game board

- **A1:** Place the Mentat on its side in the Mentat space.
- **A2:** Place the four Alliance tokens on the marked areas of the Faction Influence tracks (Emperor, Spacing Guild, Bene Gesserit, and Fremen).

### B. Make the Conflict Deck

- Separate the Conflict cards by their backs: Conflict I, Conflict II, and Conflict III.
- Shuffle the four Conflict III cards. Place all of them face down in the marked area.
- Shuffle the ten Conflict II cards. Deal five of them face down on top of the Conflict III cards.
- Shuffle the four Conflict I cards. Deal one of them face down on top of the Conflict II cards.
- You now have a 10-card Conflict Deck: one Conflict I card on top, five Conflict II cards below it, then four Conflict III cards at the bottom.
- Return the unused Conflict cards to the box. Do not look at them.

### C. Prepare cards at the edge of the board

- **C1:** Shuffle the Intrigue Deck. Place it face down.
- **C2:** Shuffle the Imperium Deck. Place it face down. Deal five cards face up from it to form the Imperium Row.
- **C3:** Next to the Imperium Row, place the Reserve cards in three stacks: Arrakis Liaison, The Spice Must Flow, and Foldspace.

### D. Select Leaders

- Each player takes a Leader and places it in front of them. You may select or select at random.
- Leaders show one to three icons after their names. Leaders with more icons are more complex to play.
- For your first game, each player must select a Leader with one icon: Paul Atreides, Glossu “The Beast” Rabban, Earl Memnon Thorvald, or Count Ilban Richese.

### E. Prepare starting decks

- Each player takes a 10-card starting deck, shuffles it, and places it face down in their supply to the left of their Leader.

### F. Prepare resources

- **F1:** Each player takes 1 water and places it in their supply.
- **F2:** Make a bank next to the board with Solari, spice, and remaining water tokens. These tokens are not limited.

### G. Select colors and place player pieces

- Each player selects a color and takes all pieces of that color.
- **G1:** Place two Agents on your Leader. Place your third Agent (your Swordmaster) next to the board.
- **G2:** Place one disc (Score marker) on the Score track. In a 4-player game, place it on space 1. In other games, place it on space 0.
- **G3:** Place your Combat marker (with the shown side face up) on space 0 of the Combat track.
- **G4:** Place four cubes, one each, on the bottom spaces of the four Faction Influence tracks.
- **G5:** The other 12 cubes are your troops. Place three troops in one of the four circular garrisons on the board (each player takes the garrison closest to them).
- **G6:** Place your remaining pieces in your supply, where all players can see them.

### H. Select the first player

- Select a first player at random. That player takes the First Player marker.
- Solo and Two-Player games need more setup. See the House Hagal rules sheet.

Tip: To improve your experience, use the Dire Wolf Game Room companion app on PC, phone, or tablet.

## 5. Game Objective and Major Concepts

### 5.1 Objective

You are a Leader of a Great House of the Landsraad.
Fight your rivals in Combat. Use political Influence. Make alliances with the four major Factions.
Victory Points measure your success.

When you gain or lose a Victory Point, move your Score marker up or down one space on the Score track.
At the end of a round, if a player has 10 or more Victory Points (or if the Conflict Deck is empty), the game ends.
The player with the most Victory Points wins.

### 5.2 Leaders

Each Leader has two different special abilities:

- The first ability, on the left, is used during play as described on the Leader.
- The second ability, on the right and marked with the Signet Ring icon, activates when you play your Signet Ring card on one of your Agent turns.

### 5.3 Deck Building

You start with a 10-card deck. All players start with the same cards.
To add to and change that deck is the key part of a deck-building game.
During each round, you may acquire new cards for your deck.
Because cards do different things, each player’s deck and plan will differ as the game continues.

When you acquire a new Imperium or Reserve card, first place it in your discard pile.
When you cannot draw a card because your deck is empty, reshuffle your discard pile to make a new deck. Then continue to draw as needed.

Some effects let you “trash” cards. This removes them from your deck for the rest of the game.
If you remove weak cards from your deck, you draw your strong cards more frequently.

Cards of any type can override the usual rules of the game.

### 5.4 Agents

You start with two Agents. You can gain a third Agent during play.
Send Agents to spaces on the board. Some spaces give resources. Some spaces require resources to advance your plan.
See Phase 2: Player Turns for full rules. See the Board Space Guide sheet for details of each board space.

In DUNE: IMPERIUM, Agents and cards connect tightly.
You cannot send an Agent to a board space without first playing a card that permits it.

**The Mentat Agent:**
The Mentat is a special freelance Agent.
You can add it to your Agents for one round.
See the Mentat board space in the Board Space Guide for more details.

## 6. Factions

In DUNE: IMPERIUM, the four Factions are strong powers on Dune and in the Imperium.
To increase your Influence and to earn an Alliance with one or more Factions is a key path to victory.
See “Across the Imperium” for short descriptions of each Faction.

Rules for Influence:

- Your Faction cubes start at the bottom of each Faction Influence track.
- During the game, your Influence increases or decreases, based on cards you play and actions you take.
- When you send an Agent to a Faction board space, gain one Influence with that Faction. Move your cube up one space on its track.
- Other game effects can also move your cube up (and sometimes down) the track.
- When you reach 2 Influence with a Faction, you gain 1 Victory Point. If you fall below 2 Influence, you lose that Victory Point.
- When you reach 4 Influence, you gain the bonus shown on that space. If you fall below 4 Influence, you keep the bonus.
- It is possible (but unusual) to earn the same bonus more than one time, if you move back and then advance again.
- The first player to reach 4 Influence with a Faction also earns an Alliance with that Faction. Take the Alliance token from the track, put it in your supply, and gain the Victory Point shown on the token.
- If an opponent later moves to a higher space on the track and passes you, give the Alliance token to that opponent. You lose that Victory Point and the opponent gains it.

## 7. Intrigue Cards

Intrigue cards represent secrets, deals, and surprise events.
They can give resources such as water or spice, increase your Influence with a Faction, or even give Victory Points.
Each Intrigue card states when you can play it, what effect it has, and what costs or conditions apply.

You get Intrigue cards primarily from three board spaces: Carthag, Conspire, and Secrets.
But look for other chances on cards, where you see the Intrigue card icon.
Keep Intrigue cards face down, separate from your deck. You may look at them at any time.
Show them to opponents only when you play them.
After you play and resolve an Intrigue card, place it face up in a discard pile next to the Intrigue Deck.

There are three types of Intrigue cards:

- **Plot:** You may play it at any time during one of your Agent or Reveal turns.
- **Combat:** You may play it only during Combat.
- **Endgame:** You may play it only at the end of the game.

## 8. Round Structure

DUNE: IMPERIUM is played in a series of rounds. Each round has five phases, in this order:

1. Round Start
2. Player Turns (Agent Turn or Reveal Turn)
3. Combat
4. Makers
5. Recall

### 8.1 Phase 1: Round Start

- Reveal a new Conflict card from the top of the Conflict Deck. Place it face up in the space next to the Conflict Deck (on top of Conflict cards from past rounds).
- Then each player draws five cards from their own deck to form their hand for the round.

### 8.2 Phase 2: Player Turns — Overview

- Start with the player who has the First Player marker. Continue clockwise. Players take one turn at a time.
- On your turn, take either an Agent turn or a Reveal turn. See next sections for details.
- Usually, you take Agent turns until you have no Agents to place. Then you take a Reveal turn.
- Note: Agent turns are optional. You can take a Reveal turn even if you still have Agents.
- After you take a Reveal turn, your turns are skipped for the rest of the phase. Other players continue to take turns.
- After all players take a Reveal turn, this phase ends.
- You may play Plot Intrigue cards during this phase, at any time during your own Agent or Reveal turn.

### 8.3 Card Anatomy

Each card in your deck has two parts: an Agent box and a Reveal box.
On a given turn, you may use only one box: the Agent box during an Agent turn, or the Reveal box during a Reveal turn.

- **A. Name**
- **B. Faction** — Not all cards belong to a Faction.
- **C. Agent icons**
- **D. Agent box**
- **E. Reveal box**
- **F. Persuasion Cost** — at top right.
- **G. Acquire effect** — Not all cards have it. See Icon Guide for Acquire.

## 9. Player Turn — Agent Turn

In an Agent turn, play one card from your hand face up in front of you.
Use it to send an Agent from your Leader to an empty space on the board.

Rules:

- The board space must show an icon in its upper left corner that matches one Agent icon on the card.
- Select only one Agent icon on your card. One card cannot send more than one Agent.
- You cannot send an Agent to a board space that already has an Agent.
- You must pay all costs and meet all requirements of the board space you select.
- In addition, the Agent box of the card you played may give an effect when you play it on an Agent turn. Ignore the Reveal box during Agent turns.
- If a card has no Agent icons, you cannot play it during an Agent turn. Reveal it only during a Reveal turn.

The seven Agent icons are:

- Emperor
- Spacing Guild
- Bene Gesserit
- Fremen
- Landsraad
- City
- Spice Trade

Example: Sardaukar Legion card has two Agent icons: Emperor and Landsraad.
The Emperor icon lets you send your Agent to either of the two Emperor board spaces.
The Landsraad icon lets you send it to one of the five Landsraad board spaces instead.

### 9.1 Board Space Costs

To send an Agent to some board spaces, you must pay a cost.
If you cannot pay the cost now (before you resolve effects of the space or the card), you cannot send your Agent there.

Example: The two Emperor spaces. The top space “Conspire” requires 4 spice.
The bottom space “Wealth” has no extra cost.

### 9.2 Effects when you play a card

When you play a card and send an Agent to a board space, you gain:
1) the effects of the board space, and
2) the effects in the Agent box of the card.
If the board space belongs to a Faction, also move your cube up one space on its Influence track.
You may do all these effects in any order.

Some card effects use text, but many use icons.
See the Board Space Guide for all board spaces. See the back cover of the rulebook for all game icons and terms.

Arrows on cards and on the board show a cost (left of or above the arrow) and an effect (right of or below the arrow).
You are never forced to pay such a cost. But if you do not pay, you do not gain the effect.

Example: When you play Fremen Camp, you may decide not to pay 2 spice to recruit 3 troops.

### 9.3 Sietch Tabr Influence

The Sietch Tabr board space has a special requirement.
To send an Agent there, you must have 2 or more Influence with the Fremen.

## 10. Player Turn — Agent Turn (Continued)

### 10.1 Deploy troops to the Conflict

At the start of each round, the current Conflict card is revealed. It offers rewards for Combat.

When the cube icon appears on a card or board space, you recruit one troop.
Take a troop from your supply and place it in your garrison on the board.
If you have no troops in your supply, you cannot recruit more until some return there.

Troops in your garrison earn no rewards.
You may deploy them to a Conflict when you send an Agent to a Combat space.
Combat spaces have a desert picture and crossed swords.
Most Combat spaces are on Dune itself, but three are with Factions: Heighliner with the Spacing Guild, and Hardy Warriors and Stillsuits with the Fremen.

When you send an Agent to a Combat space, you may deploy troops to the Conflict area (the area in the center of the garrisons).
You may deploy any or all troops recruited during your current turn (from both the board space and the card you played), plus up to two more troops from your garrison.
Place troops you recruit but do not deploy in your garrison as usual.

### 10.2 Control Bonuses

Some Conflict cards (see Phase 3: Combat) reward control of one of three spaces on Dune: Arrakeen, Carthag, or Imperial Basin (based on the card title).
If you win such a Conflict, take a Control marker from your supply and place it on the flag below the correct board space.

While your Control marker is on one of these spaces, you receive the shown bonus each time any player (including you) sends an Agent there.
The bonus is 1 Solari for Arrakeen or Carthag, and 1 spice for Imperial Basin.

When a Conflict card is revealed for a space that you already control, you receive a defensive bonus: you may deploy one troop from your supply to the Conflict.

### 10.3 Harvest spice

Three board spaces on Arrakis have a Maker icon, where you may harvest spice: The Great Flat, Hagga Basin, and Imperial Basin.
When you send an Agent to one of them, you gain the base spice shown on the space, plus any bonus spice that has collected there. See Phase 4: Makers.

Example: When you send an Agent to Hagga Basin, you gain 2 spice from the bank and take all bonus spice there.

### 10.4 Agent turn examples

- **John** plays Dune, the Desert Planet to send an Agent to Imperial Basin. He collects spice from the space. His card gives no extra Agent effect.
- Imperial Basin is a Combat space. John recruited no troops with the card or the space. But he has three troops in his garrison. He deploys two troops from his garrison to the Conflict (the maximum number).
- **Abby** plays Duncan Idaho to send an Agent to Carthag. John’s Control marker is there from a past round, so John takes 1 Solari from the bank.
- From the space, Abby recruits a troop and draws an Intrigue card. With Duncan Idaho, she pays 1 water to recruit one more troop and draw one card from her deck.
- Abby could place her two new troops in her garrison. But Carthag is also a Combat space, and she may beat John. She deploys those troops to the Conflict, plus one more from her garrison. If she had a second troop there, she could deploy it too.
- **Ned** wants to save forces for a later round. He plays Bene Gesserit Initiate to send an Agent to Rally Troops. He must pay the space cost of 4 Solari. From the card, he draws a card. From the space, he recruits four troops. It is not a Combat space, so he cannot deploy troops to the Conflict. He places them in his garrison instead.

## 11. Player Turn — Reveal Turn

When you have no more Agents for Agent turns (or you decide not to use remaining Agents), take a Reveal turn.
Do these steps in order: Reveal Cards, Resolve Reveal Effects, Set Strength, and Clean Up.

### 11.1 Reveal Cards

Reveal all cards that remain in your hand. Place them face up in play in front of you.
Keep them separate from cards you played on past Agent turns.

### 11.2 Resolve Reveal Effects

Gain the effects in the Reveal boxes of all cards you just revealed (but not cards you played on Agent turns past in the round).
You may resolve Reveal effects in any order.
You may use Persuasion that you gained to acquire new cards for your deck before, between, or after your Reveal effects.

### 11.3 Set Strength

Total your strength for Combat this round.

- Each troop you have in the Conflict is worth 2 strength. Troops in your garrison or supply give nothing.
- Each sword you revealed during your Reveal turn is worth 1 strength.
- You must have at least one troop in the Conflict to have strength. If your last troop is removed, your strength becomes 0. It cannot increase by any means, including swords on revealed cards.
- When you total your strength, tell your opponents and move your Combat marker to the correct space on the Combat track.
- If your strength is more than 20, flip the Combat marker to the +20 side and start again from the start of the track.

### 11.4 Clean Up

Remove all cards from in front of you (from Agent and Reveal turns) and put them in your discard pile.

### 11.5 Acquire cards

- Use Persuasion gained during a round during your Reveal turn to acquire new cards for your deck.
- You may acquire any of the five cards in the Imperium Row, or Arrakis Liaison or The Spice Must Flow from the Reserve. (Acquire Foldspace at the board space of the same name, as described in the Board Space Guide.)
- The cost to acquire a card is shown at the top right of that card.
- You may acquire as many cards as you want, if you have sufficient Persuasion.
- You may combine Persuasion from more than one source to acquire one card. You may split Persuasion from one source to pay for different cards.
- Persuasion has no tokens because you cannot save it. Persuasion you do not use during your Reveal turn is lost.
- When you acquire a card, place it in your discard pile (face up to the right of your Leader). You cannot use the new card now. You will shuffle it with the rest of your discard pile when you run out of cards to draw.
- The Imperium Row must always have five cards. When it does not, replace missing cards from the top of the Imperium Deck. Thus after you acquire one card, you may then acquire the card that replaces it (if you have sufficient Persuasion).

### 11.6 Reveal turn example

John has no more Agents when his turn comes, so he takes a Reveal turn.
He reveals the three cards that remain in his hand: Imperial Spy, Smuggler’s Thopter, and Stilgar.

- Smuggler’s Thopter has an effect to resolve. He takes 1 spice from the bank. His cards also give Persuasion and swords.
- John’s cards give him a total of 4 Persuasion. He uses 3 Persuasion to acquire Space Travel from the Imperium Row and places it in his discard pile. A new card is then dealt from the Imperium Deck to fill the empty space. He has 1 Persuasion left, but no cards cost only 1.
- John has two troops in the Conflict, worth 4 strength. The revealed cards give 4 more: 1 sword from Imperial Spy and 3 swords from Stilgar. He sets his Combat marker at 8 on the Combat track.
- Finally, John puts the cards in front of him in his discard pile.
- The phase continues. Abby and Ned will take a Reveal turn later. John is done until the Combat phase.

## 12. Phase 3: Combat

Resolve Combat during this phase. But first, players may play Combat Intrigue cards.

### 12.1 Combat Intrigue Cards

- Start with the player who has the First Player marker. Continue clockwise. Each player with at least one troop in the Conflict may play any number of Combat Intrigue cards, or may pass.
- You are not required to pass only because you passed past in this phase. After all players in Combat pass in sequence, resolve the Combat.
- If a card changes the number of troops a player has in the Conflict (or changes strength), adjust the Combat marker on the Combat track. Remember: if you have no troops in the Conflict, your strength is 0.
- Some Combat Intrigue cards do an effect “when you win a Conflict.” Do not play these now, before you resolve Combat. Wait until you win, then play them (before the next phase).

### 12.2 Resolve Combat

Give rewards from the Conflict card to players based on strength, as shown on the Combat track.

- The player with the highest strength wins the Conflict and gains the top reward on the Conflict card.
- The player with the second highest strength gains the second reward. In a 4-player game (only), the player with the third highest strength gains the third reward.
- A player with 0 strength receives no reward.
- See the back cover of this rulebook for icons used on Conflict cards.
- After all rewards are given, each player takes their troops from the Conflict and puts them in their supply (not their garrison). Reset all Combat markers to 0 on the Combat track.

### 12.3 Ties

- When players tie for first place, no one wins the Conflict. The tied players each receive the second reward. (In a 4-player game, if two players tie for first place, the other two players still compete for the third reward.)
- When players tie for second place, they each receive the third reward.
- Players that tie for third place receive nothing.

### 12.4 Combat example

As Combat starts, John has 8 strength and Abby has 6 strength.
John has the First Player marker, but he is already ahead. He passes.

- Abby is next and plays Ambush. It gives her 4 more strength. She moves her Combat marker to 10.
- Ned is next, but has no troops in the Conflict. He has 0 strength and cannot take part.
- It is now John again. He passed past, but he could now play an Intrigue card. But none of his Intrigue cards are Combat cards. He passes again.
- Abby, satisfied with victory, also passes.
- With 10 strength, Abby wins the Conflict. The top reward of Siege of Arrakeen gives her 1 Victory Point and control of Arrakeen. She moves her Score marker one space and places a Control marker on the flag below Arrakeen.
- John wins the second-place reward: 4 Solari from the bank.
- Ned receives nothing. He has 0 strength, and the third-place reward is not available in a 3-player game.

## 13. Phase 4: Makers

In this phase, spice can collect on some board spaces.
Check each of the three board spaces with a Maker icon: The Great Flat, Hagga Basin, and Imperial Basin.
If the space has no Agent, place 1 spice from the bank on that space (in the spot for bonus spice).
Add this spice to bonus spice that may already be there from past rounds.

Example: There is an Agent in Imperial Basin (John played it there past in the round).
There are no Agents in the Great Flat or Hagga Basin, so each of those spaces gets 1 bonus spice.
The Great Flat already had 1 bonus spice from a past round, so now it has 2 bonus spice.

---

## 14. Phase 5: Recall

If a player has 10 or more Victory Points on the Score track, or if the Conflict Deck is empty, start the Endgame.

If no one has won, prepare the next round:

- Return the Mentat to its space in the Landsraad (if it is not already there).
- Players recall their Agents and return them to their Leaders.
- Pass the First Player marker clockwise to the next player. Then start a new round with Phase 1.

Example: No one has 10 Victory Points, so return the Mentat and the Agents to the correct places. Pass the First Player marker clockwise to Abby. Start a new round with Phase 1.

### 14.1 Endgame

- First, you may play and resolve any Endgame Intrigue cards you have.
- Then the player with the most Victory Points wins.
- In case of a tie, use tiebreakers in this order: amount of spice, Solari, water, and garrisoned troops.

## 15. Clarifications

- **Baron Vladimir Harkonnen — Masterstroke:** To use it, select two of the four Baron Harkonnen tokens and place them on your Leader with the Faction side face down. Return the other two to the box without letting opponents see them. Later, reveal your selections as described to gain your reward.
- **Bindu Suspension:** You must play it at the start of your Agent turn, before you do anything else. When you do, draw a card, then end your turn at once.
- **Calculated Hire:** This card can only take the Mentat from its start position in the Landsraad.
- **Demand Respect:** If you win a Conflict that rewards spice, you may take that spice first, then use it to play this card. (You may also play To the Victor… to gain spice and then play Demand Respect.)
- **Kwisatz Haderach:** You may play it to take an Agent turn even if you have no Agents left, as it lets you send an Agent that is already on a board space. You may send an Agent back to the space it was just on. If a board space can be used only one time per game (High Council, Swordmaster), you cannot use Kwisatz Haderach to send an Agent there more than one time. You cannot overcome The Voice and send an Agent to a space where you cannot send an Agent. If you move an Agent away from a space with a Maker icon, and that space stays empty until Phase 4, bonus spice does collect there.
- **Paul Atreides / Poison Snooper:** You cannot look at the top card of your deck if it is empty. (You cannot reshuffle your discard pile to make a new deck until you need to draw a card.)
- **Test of Humanity:** Opponents make selections starting from your left and going clockwise. Each opponent must select an action they can do. They cannot discard if their hand is empty, or lose a deployed troop if they have none. An opponent who discards selects which card; it is not random.
- **The Voice:** Use one of your free Control markers to mark the board space you select, then remove it on your next turn. (The Voice only stops opponents from sending Agents to that space for one turn.) This does stop Kwisatz Haderach from sending an Agent to the selected space.

For latest rulings, see FAQ at: http://www.duneimperium.com/FAQ

## 16. Strategy Tips

- Water is rare and precious on Dune, and vital to harvest spice. Look to the Fremen if you need water.
- Spice has many uses. Surplus spice always finds a buyer. CHOAM or the Emperor will exchange it for Solari. The Spacing Guild only deals in very large amounts of spice, but if you meet their needs, their heighliner ships move large numbers of troops to Dune with ease.
- Solari is the currency of the Imperium and helps the Landsraad Council work. Whether you want a Swordmaster or High Council seat, or only need extra troops or a Mentat now, do not come without Solari.
- When you acquire cards for your deck, part of the cost buys access to Factions. Keep the correct tools to send Agents to key board spaces.
- Each Faction has one board space with a cost and one without a cost. Spaces without a cost still help you gain Influence while you collect resources for the other spaces.
- To win Conflicts, position is key. The longer you wait to commit troops, the more data you have when you do so. Opponents then have less chance to react.
- Sometimes a small victory at low cost is more valuable than a large victory at high cost.
- Intrigue cards mean you can never be certain how a Conflict will end.
- An Alliance with a Faction can be a strong asset on your path to victory. Watch for other players who try to race past you. Do not miss a chance to do the same to them.
- The secretive Bene Gesserit are a good source of Intrigue cards, which let you surprise opponents. They also can trash cards in your deck. This improves your deck and helps you find your strongest cards faster and more frequently.

## 18. Icon Guide and Additional Terms

- **Acquire:** Some cards have a special acquire box, shown below their cost. You gain the effect here one time, at the moment you acquire the card (and not later, when you play it).
- **Acquire Foldspace:** Acquire a Foldspace card from the Reserve.
- **Agent:** From the Swordmaster space, you can gain your third Agent (that was placed next to the board during setup). Then use it for the rest of the game.
- **Alliance:** You may use this effect only if you have the Alliance token of the shown Faction (for example, an Alliance with the Spacing Guild).
- **Control:** If you win a Conflict with a Control reward (of Arrakeen, Carthag, or Imperial Basin), place your Control marker on the flag below that space (replace any opponent marker there). When a Conflict card is revealed for a space you already control, you may deploy one troop from your supply to the Conflict.
- **Draw a card:** Draw a card from your deck. If your deck is empty, reshuffle the discard pile to make a new deck, then continue to draw.
- **Draw Intrigue:** Draw an Intrigue card from the Intrigue Deck. Keep it face down until you play it. You may look at it at any time.
- **Fremen Bond:** You may use this effect if you have one or more other Fremen cards in play. Two cards with Fremen Bond can activate each other, regardless of order played. See In Play.
- **In Play:** Cards you play on Agent turns and reveal during your Reveal turn stay face up and “in play” until you clean up at the end of your Reveal turn (unless trashed first).
- **Influence requirement:** You may use this effect only if you have at least the shown Influence with the shown Faction (for example, 2 or more Influence with the Fremen).
- **Gain Influence:** Gain Influence with the shown Faction: Emperor, Spacing Guild, Bene Gesserit, or Fremen.
- **Gain one / gain two / lose one Influence:** Select any one of the four Factions. (When you gain two Influence, you cannot select two different Factions.)
- **Lose a troop:** When you lose a troop, return it to your supply (not your garrison).
- **Maker:** The three board spaces with the Maker icon each gain 1 bonus spice during Phase 4: Makers if no Agent is present. When you send an Agent to one of these spaces, you also gain all bonus spice there.
- **Mentat:** Take the Mentat from its space on the board (if it is there; you cannot take it from a player or a different space). Place it on your Leader; you may use it as one of your Agents to take an Agent turn. When you take the Mentat as a Conflict reward, keep it as an Agent for the next round and do not return it to the board at the end of this round.
- **Pay a cost:** An arrow shows a cost (left of or above the arrow) and an effect (right of or below the arrow). If you do not pay the cost, you do not get the effect. You are never forced to pay such a cost on a card.
- **Persuasion (in shown amount):** You get Persuasion mainly from Reveal boxes on your cards. Use it to acquire Imperium or Reserve cards. Pay the cost shown at the top right of a card.
- **Recall:** When you recall one of your Agents, put it back on your Leader. You may use it again on a different Agent turn in the same round.
- **Resources: Solari, Spice, Water:** When you gain a resource or pay one as a cost, take it from or return it to the bank. (For Solari and Spice, gain or pay the shown amount.)
- **Retreat:** When you retreat a troop, move it from the Conflict back to your garrison.
- **Signet Ring:** When you play your Signet Ring card on an Agent turn, you use the Signet Ring ability (with the same icon) on your Leader.
- **Steal Intrigue:** Each opponent who has four or more Intrigue cards must give you one of them (selected at random).
- **Sword:** Each sword adds 1 strength to your strength total in a Conflict.
- **Trash:** Trash one card from your hand, discard pile, or in play. Return it to the box; it is not used for the rest of the game. (Return Reserve cards to their stack in the Reserve instead.) Trashing is optional unless it pays a cost, or if a card tells you to trash itself.
- **Troop:** Recruit one troop; take it from your supply and put it in your garrison. (If you recruited the troop while you sent an Agent to a Combat space, you may deploy it to the Conflict.)
- **Victory Point:** When you gain a Victory Point, move your Score marker one space up on the Score track. When you lose one, move it one space down.

## 20. House Hagal Cards — Solo and Two-Player Games

House Hagal cards control automated opponents (called “Rivals”).
Rivals compete against you in Solo and Two-Player games.
They send Agents to occupy board spaces. They contest Conflicts by recruiting and deploying troops.

Rivals are affected by cards and board spaces that affect opponents (or “players”), if they have the stated resources.

Tip: To improve your experience, use the Dire Wolf Game Room app on PC, phone, or tablet. You can use it in place of physical House Hagal cards.

### 20.1 Rival Agents

During Phase 2: Player Turns, a Rival takes Agent turns but does not take Reveal turns.
When a Rival takes an Agent turn, reveal the top card of the House Hagal deck.
That card sends an Agent to the shown board space, if that space is empty.
If the space is occupied, ignore the card and continue to reveal cards until you reveal an empty space.
If the House Hagal deck is ever empty (or if the Reshuffle card is revealed), at once reshuffle the House Hagal cards to make a new deck.

When a Rival sends an Agent to a board space, ignore all normal costs and effects of that space.
Instead, it gets only the effects shown on its revealed card:

- The Rival advances on the shown Influence track. It earns no bonuses from a track, but it takes a Faction Alliance if able.
- The Rival uses the Signet Ring ability on its Leader. (Solo Play only)
- The Rival recruits one troop from its supply for each troop icon. If the revealed card is a Combat space, deploy them to the Conflict. If not, put the troops in its garrison.
- **Harvest Spice cards:** If the revealed card is a Harvest Spice card, obey the instructions on the card. If the card sends a Rival Agent to harvest spice, remove all bonus spice on the space. (In a Two-Player game, return it to the bank. In Solo Play, obey Solo Play rules for Harvest Spice.)

Any time a Rival sends an Agent to a Combat space, it also deploys up to two troops from its garrison (if free) to the Conflict.
Do this even if the card itself recruited no troops (for example: a Harvest Spice card).

### 20.2 Rival Combat

When Phase 3: Combat starts, each Rival that has at least one troop in the Conflict gets a Combat bonus.
In turn order, reveal the top card of the House Hagal deck.
(If you reveal the Reshuffle card, obey instructions to reshuffle the deck and reveal again.)
Ignore all items on the revealed card except sword icons at the bottom.
Advance the Rival Combat marker on the Combat track by the number of swords revealed.
Players may then play Combat Intrigue cards before Combat is resolved.

### 20.3 Two-Player Games

In a Two-Player game, a third Rival player, House Hagal, competes against you and your opponent. It has a spoiler role.
It operates by its own rules. It does not collect resources, build a deck, earn rewards, or score Victory Points.
But it uses House Hagal cards as described above. It sends Agents to occupy spaces and competes in Conflicts by recruiting and deploying troops.

**Setup:**

- Select a color for House Hagal. Place one cube on each bottom space below the four Faction Influence tracks. Put the rest of its cubes in its supply. It starts with no troops in its garrison.
- Remove from the House Hagal deck the three Arrakeen cards marked “1P” in the upper right corner. Shuffle the remaining cards and place them and House Hagal’s three Agents near its supply.

**During the game:**

- After each of the First Player’s Agent turns, House Hagal acts, if it has an Agent left in its supply. (Use the method described above.) The number of turns House Hagal takes each round changes because it acts after the First Player. For example, if one player has a Swordmaster and the other does not, it can take three turns in one round, then two in the next.
- Because House Hagal cannot collect rewards from a Conflict, it can never control a board space. But if it wins a Conflict for a space now controlled by a different player, remove that player’s Control marker.

### 20.4 Solo Play

In a Solo game, you fight two Rivals who (again) do not use the normal rules.
Neither builds a deck, but they have other strong advantages. They share your goal: gain favor with the most powerful factions and control the spice trade on Arrakis.
If either Rival reaches 10 or more Victory Points, they start the end of the game — and they may beat you.

**Setup:**

- Select a difficulty level from the chart below.
- Select two Leaders, one for each Rival. During the game, these Rivals use only the Signet ability on their Leaders. Ignore the ability on the left. Rivals cannot play Paul Atreides or Helena Richese. (For your first solo game, Earl Memnon Thorvald and Glossu “The Beast” Rabban are recommended.)
- For each Rival, select a color. Place one cube on each bottom space below the four Faction Influence tracks. Put the stated number of troops in their garrison for your difficulty, and the rest in their supply. Place two Agents in their supply, and insert the Swordmasters (third Agents) for both Rivals into the Conflict deck. Put exactly the stated number of cards on top of them for your difficulty.
- Remove from the House Hagal deck all cards marked “2P” in the upper right corner (the Reshuffle card and three Arrakeen cards). Shuffle the remaining cards and place the deck near your Rivals.
- If your difficulty requires it, place a 5 Solari token on the Mentat space. For this game, this space costs 5 Solari instead of 2.
- You and your Rivals each start with 1 water, plus extra resources stated for your difficulty.
- The Rival on your left takes the First Player marker.

**Difficulty chart — Setup changes by difficulty:**

- Mercenary (Novice) / Sardaukar (Veteran) / Mentat (Expert):
  - Your Extra Starting Resources: 1 Solari + 1 Spice / — / —
  - 5 Solari Token on Mentat Space: No / Yes / Yes
  - Rival Starting Garrisoned Troops: — / 3 / 3
  - Conflict Cards Above Rival Swordmasters: 5 / 4 / 3
  - Rival Extra Starting Resources: — / 1 Intrigue card / 1 Intrigue card
- Kwisatz Haderach (Expert+): You also cannot gain a Swordmaster.

**Rivals’ Turns:**

- During Phase 2: Player Turns, your Rivals each take Agent turns in sequence with you, while they have an Agent left in supply. (Use the method described above.)
- When a Rival uses Harvest Spice to send an Agent to a board space, it gains all spice there (base and bonus). Place all collected resources in the Rival’s supply.

**Combat:**

Rivals gain first- and second-place rewards from Conflicts, even items they do not take from board spaces:

- They gain Victory Points and Influence with Factions.
- They gain Solari, Spice, Water, and Intrigue cards. Keep these in the Rival’s supply (to be spent as stated below).
- They win Control of board spaces and place a Control marker on the flag below it. On future turns, they get the Control bonus each time you or a Rival sends an Agent there. They also receive the defensive bonus of one troop if a Conflict over that space is later revealed.
- They gain the Mentat, and use it as an Agent during the next round.

**Additional rules for Rivals:**

- **Swordmasters:** When you reveal the Conflict card above the Rival Swordmasters, your Rivals each at once receive their third Agents. They use them in the current round and for the rest of the game.
- **Choices:** When a Rival has a choice about gaining Influence, it selects the Faction in which it has the least Influence. In case of a tie (or when there is a choice not about Influence), you decide for the Rival.
- **Corner the Market:** For this Intrigue card, treat each Rival as if they have two The Spice Must Flow cards. (You need three yourself to get 2 Victory Points.)
- **Expert Troop Deployment:** When you play at Expert difficulty, your Rivals are more selective about deploying troops to a Conflict. When they fight for a Conflict I or II card, a Rival does not deploy troops if it already leads by two or more troops (it keeps them in its garrison instead). For a critical Conflict III card, a Rival deploys all troops it can at each chance.
- **Spending Resources:** At any time, when a Rival has the stated resources (7 of each type as shown on Rival aid), they spend them to earn a Victory Point.
